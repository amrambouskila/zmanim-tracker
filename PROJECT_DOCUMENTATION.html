Overview — what this is & why

A safety-critical halachic tool: people rely on these times for davening, Shabbat candle lighting, and havdalah. Accuracy is treated like a correctness invariant, not a nicety.

zmanim-tracker computes zmanim — the halachically-defined times of day that govern Jewish prayer and observance. Unlike fixed clock times, most zmanim are derived from the sun's position relative to the horizon at a specific latitude, longitude, and date. The app takes a location (entered as lat/lon, a US ZIP code, or free text) and a date range, then produces every standard zman per day: dawn (alos) at several depression angles, misheyakir, sunrise, latest Shema and Shacharit, chatzos, the three Mincha boundaries, plag hamincha, sunset, nightfall (tzais), chatzot halaila, plus Shabbat candle lighting and havdalah on the relevant days.

The core idea is the proportional halachic hour (shaah zmanis): daylight from sunrise to sunset is divided into twelve equal parts, so an "hour" is longer in summer and shorter in winter. Time-based zmanim are expressed as multiples of that hour from sunrise; angle-based zmanim (dawn/dusk) come straight from the sun's depression below the horizon. The current implementation uses the GRA opinion for proportional times and a configurable set of depression angles for the twilight boundaries.

This is Phase 1: a single-user Streamlit app that runs entirely locally (solar math via the astral library; offline ZIP and timezone lookups), with free-text geocoding the only network dependency. The codebase has been refactored out of an original single-file prototype into a clean one-class-per-file src/ module tree with a full pytest suite gated at 100% coverage. Phase 2 (FastAPI + React + Hebrew calendar + Postgres) and Phase 3 (notifications, calendar sync, PDF) are planned but explicitly not built yet.

Key concept
A zman is a halachic time boundary computed from sun geometry. The whole app is a pipeline: resolve a location → compute solar primitives → derive proportional and angle-based zmanim → tabulate / chart / export. Everything else is plumbing around that core.

Architecture

A linear, stateless pipeline. The UI orchestrates four collaborators; the engine is the only place halachic math lives, and it depends on nothing but the solar solver and the data models.

Location string→Resolver→Calculator→DataBuilder→DataFrame→Table / Chart / CSV

Request sequence

When the user clicks Generate, ZmanimApp.run() drives this flow:

Process boundaries & principles

  • One process. No backend, no database, no message bus. Streamlit runs the whole pipeline in-process on each rerun.
  • Engine is standalone. ZmanimCalculatorAngleBased depends only on SolarAngleSolver and the dataclasses — it is importable and usable without Streamlit, pandas, or the resolver. This is what lets Phase 2 reuse it behind FastAPI unchanged.
  • Validation at boundaries only. The resolver validates user input and external responses (lat/lon range, empty input, Nominatim failure). Internal engine calls trust their types.
  • Timezone correctness is load-bearing. Every datetime is timezone-aware (ZoneInfo); the timezone is resolved once per location and threaded through the solar solver.

Core modules

The heart of the doc. Each drill-down covers what the module does, its key logic, and the exact files and lines.

SolarAngleSolver src/engine/solar_angle_solver.py

A stateless static-method wrapper around the astral library. It is the single point where raw astronomy enters the system. Every method builds an astral.Observer from lat/lon and asks astral for a specific solar event, returning a timezone-aware datetime in the location's IANA timezone (via ZoneInfo(tz_name)).

Five primitives are exposed: sunrise_local, sunset_local, solar_noon_local, and the two angle-parameterized ones — dawn_at_depression_local and dusk_at_depression_local. The depression methods are what make every angle-based zman possible: pass a depression in degrees and astral solves for the time the sun crosses that angle below the horizon (dawn = morning side, dusk = evening side).

Watch out
In polar regions or extreme angles the sun may never reach a given depression; astral raises ValueError in those cases. The master plan's data-flow contract says compute_for_day "raises on polar regions where sun doesn't set/rise" — that propagation is by design, not caught.
src/engine/solar_angle_solver.pyAll five solar primitives.
tests/test_solar_angle_solver.pyValidates ordering and tz-awareness of each primitive.
ZmanimCalculatorAngleBased src/engine/zmanim_calculator.py

The halachic engine. Its constructor captures the configurable opinion parameters (depression angles, candle-lighting offset, Shabbat-end basis), coercing them to the right types. The single public method, compute_for_day(loc, day) -> ZmanimRow, does the whole day's calculation.

Step 1 — solar primitives. It asks SolarAngleSolver for sunrise, sunset, and solar noon (local), plus tomorrow's sunrise (needed for halachic midnight).

Step 2 — proportional hour. The GRA shaah zmanis is simply (sunset - sunrise) / 12 — a timedelta. All time-based zmanim are sunrise + N * shaah_zmanis:

Step 3 — angle-based zmanim. Five dawn/dusk times from the configured depression angles (alos astronomical 16.9°, alos nautical 12°, misheyakir 10°, tzais three stars 8.5°, tzais civil 6°).

Step 4 — chatzot halaila. Halachic midnight is the midpoint between tonight's sunset and tomorrow's sunrise: sunset + (sunrise_tomorrow - sunset) / 2.

Step 5 — Shabbat gating. Using day.weekday() (Monday=0), Friday (4) gets candle lighting at sunset - offset; Saturday (5) gets havdalah from the configured dusk basis plus an optional offset. All other days leave both None.

Key concept
Each halachic opinion is a configuration of this one calculator, not a separate class. Changing depression angles or offsets in the constructor produces a different shitah without touching the math. This is the design that makes MGA (Phase 1 extension) an additive feature.
src/engine/zmanim_calculator.pyConstructor params + compute_for_day.
tests/test_zmanim_calculator.pyReference-validated NYC equinox case + ordering + Shabbat-gating sweep.
ZmanimDataBuilder src/engine/zmanim_data_builder.py

Turns a date range into a presentation-ready pandas.DataFrame. build(loc, start, end) validates the range (raises ValueError if end < start), walks day-by-day calling the calculator, then formats each ZmanimRow into a flat record.

Two formatting helpers do the string work: format_timestamp renders datetimes as "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S %Z" (empty string for None, so non-Shabbat days have blank candle/havdalah cells), and format_duration renders the shaah zmanis timedelta as signed HH:MM:SS.

Note
The DataFrame is the contract between engine and UI: its column names (e.g. alos_astronomical_edge, plag_hamincha, shaah_zmanis) are referenced by string in app.py and zmanim_plotter.py. Renaming a column means updating those two consumers too.
src/engine/zmanim_data_builder.pyRange loop + formatting + rows_to_df.
tests/test_zmanim_data_builder.pyRange validation, column presence, duration formatting.
LocationResolver src/location/location_resolver.py

Turns a free-form string into a Location. resolve(input) tries three strategies in priority order and always returns a valid Location (or raises ValueError); it never returns None.

  1. lat/lon — a regex matches "40.7128, -74.0060" (comma or space separated). Out-of-range coordinates raise ValueError.
  2. US ZIP — a 5-digit (optionally ZIP+4) match is resolved offline via pgeocode.Nominatim("us"); if pgeocode has no record it falls back to the geocoder.
  3. free text — anything else hits OpenStreetMap Nominatim over HTTP, taking the first result's lat/lon and display_name.

For every resolved coordinate, timezone_for calls TimezoneFinder.timezone_at and validates the result through require_iana_timezone (which constructs a ZoneInfo to prove the string is real, defaulting to "UTC" if the finder returns nothing).

Watch out
Nominatim's usage policy is ~1 request/second and requires a descriptive User-Agent. The code enforces a fixed 0.8s time.sleep before every call and sends User-Agent: Zmanim_Trackerv0.0.1. If you batch many free-text lookups, that sleep is the throttle — don't remove it.
src/location/location_resolver.pyThree resolution strategies + timezone lookup.
tests/test_location_resolver.pyMocks Nominatim (responses) and pgeocode; covers all branches and the UTC fallback.
ZmanimPlotter src/visualization/zmanim_plotter.py

Builds the interactive Plotly figure. The challenge it solves: the DataFrame stores zmanim as formatted timestamp strings, but the chart's y-axis needs to be time of day (so all curves overlay on a common 0–24h scale regardless of date). time_to_minutes parses each column's timestamp and converts it to minutes-since-midnight; the figure then plots minutes on a fixed y-axis with custom hour tick labels.

One trace per zman (16 columns in ZMANIM_PLOT_COLUMNS), each with a hover template that shows the date and the original HH:MM:SS time. The y-axis is locked to [0, 1439] minutes with hourly tickvals.

src/visualization/zmanim_plotter.pyFigure construction, minute conversion, hover formatting.
tests/test_zmanim_plotter.pyValidates trace count, minute conversion, and the HH:MM:SS formatter.
ZmanimApp (Streamlit UI) src/app.py

The orchestrator and the only UI code. Its constructor accepts optional collaborators (resolver, calculator, plotter) for testability, defaulting to real instances. run() renders the sidebar (location text input defaulting to "10001", start/end date pickers, a Generate button), and on click runs the full pipeline.

render_todays_zmanim finds today's row (using the location's local date) and lays out the available zmanim as a 3-column grid of st.metric cards, in a fixed display order (TODAYS_ZMANIM_DISPLAY_ORDER) that follows the natural progression of the day. Blank/None values (e.g. candle lighting on a weekday) are skipped.

src/app.pyZmanimApp, the display order constant, and main().
tests/test_app.pyDrives the app with fakes/stubs and asserts the render path.
Note
The if __name__ == "__main__" block is marked pragma: no cover — it's the Streamlit script entry, excluded from the coverage gate.
Dead-ish code to know about
src/models/solar_primitives_utc.py defines SolarPrimitivesUTC and has a matching test, but no engine code constructs or consumes it — the calculator calls SolarAngleSolver directly instead of going through this aggregate. It exists as a documented contract for a future refactor. If you wire it in, update ZmanimCalculatorAngleBased to build it once and read its fields.

Halachic calculations & formulas

The real math. Every formula below is implemented verbatim in src/engine/zmanim_calculator.py and documented in CLAUDE.md §5.

Shaah zmanis (proportional hour, GRA)

The daylight period is divided into 12 equal parts: shaah_zmanis = (sunset − sunrise) / 12. It is longer in summer, shorter in winter. At the equinox it is ~60.8 minutes. Every time-based zman is a multiple of this hour added to sunrise.

Time-based zmanim (GRA)

ZmanFormulaHalachic basis
Latest Shemasunrise + 3 · shaah_zmanisEnd of the 3rd halachic hour.
Latest Shacharitsunrise + 4 · shaah_zmanisEnd of the 4th hour.
Chatzossolar_noon (= sunrise + 6h)Halachic midday.
Mincha Gedolahsunrise + 6.5 · shaah_zmanisHalf-hour after chatzos; earliest Mincha.
Mincha Ketanasunrise + 9.5 · shaah_zmanisPreferred Mincha.
Plag HaMinchasunrise + 10.75 · shaah_zmanis1.25 hours before sunset.

Angle-based zmanim (depression below horizon)

ZmanDepressionMeaning
Alos (astronomical)16.9°Earliest alos hashachar (dawn).
Alos (nautical)12.0°Standard dawn.
Misheyakir10.0°Earliest time for tallit & tefillin.
Tzais (three stars)8.5°Nightfall — three medium stars visible.
Tzais (civil)6.0°Civil twilight end.

Higher depression = farther below the horizon: alos at 16.9° is earlier than at 12°; tzais at 8.5° is earlier than at 6°. The tests assert exactly this ordering.

Chatzot halaila (halachic midnight)

chatzot_halaila = sunset + (next_sunrise − sunset) / 2 — the midpoint of the night, requiring tomorrow's sunrise (which is why the engine fetches it).

Shabbat times

Candle lighting (Friday)
sunset − candle_lighting_offset_min. Default 18 minutes (standard minhag; Jerusalem commonly uses 40). Configurable.
Havdalah / Shabbat ends (Saturday)
tzais_three_stars + shabbat_end_offset_min by default, or tzais_civil_end + offset if shabbat_end_basis is set to "tzais_civil_end".
Validation reference
The engine test validates NYC (40.7128, −74.0060) on the 2024-03-20 vernal equinox against MyZmanim.com published values, with a 2-minute tolerance (solar-calc precision from refraction models). Reference EDT values: sunrise ~06:59, sunset ~19:09, solar noon ~13:04, shaah zmanis ~60.8 min, latest Shema ~10:02, latest Shacharit ~11:03. Other accepted sources per CLAUDE.md: Chabad.org and KosherJava.
MGA opinion — planned
The Magen Avraham (MGA) opinion uses a longer day: shaah_zmanis_mga = (sunset+72min − (sunrise−72min)) / 12. It is documented as a Phase 1 extension but not yet implemented. Adding it means a second config path through the same calculator, not a new class.

How to run it

Two ways: the Docker launcher (recommended) or a local uv run.

Docker (recommended)

  1. Run run_zmanim_tracker.sh on macOS/Linux, or run_zmanim_tracker.bat on Windows.
  2. The launcher runs docker compose up --build -d, polls the URL until Streamlit responds (up to 60s), then opens the browser.
  3. Open http://localhost:5270 (override with ZT_PORT).
  4. Use the menu loop: k stop/keep image · q stop/remove image · v stop/remove image+volumes · r full restart.

Local (no Docker)

  1. uv sync to install dependencies from pyproject.toml / uv.lock.
  2. uv run streamlit run src/app.py to launch the UI.
  3. Open the Streamlit URL it prints (default Streamlit port 8501 when run directly).

The README's "default port 5270" applies to the Docker mapping; a bare local Streamlit run uses 8501 unless you pass --server.port.

How to edit & extend it

Owner-focused recipes. The golden rule from CLAUDE.md: one concept per file, full type annotations, from __future__ import annotations at the top, and any calculation change must be validated against a reference source and have a test.

Add a new zman

A new zman touches four places, in order:

  1. Add the field to src/models/zmanim_row.py (with its type and a units comment).
  2. Compute it in compute_for_day and pass it into the ZmanimRow(...) construction.
  3. Emit a column in rows_to_df (use format_timestamp or format_duration).
  4. Add the column name to ZMANIM_PLOT_COLUMNS in zmanim_plotter.py and to TODAYS_ZMANIM_DISPLAY_ORDER in app.py if it should show in the chart / today view.
  5. Add a reference-validated test in tests/test_zmanim_calculator.py.

Add the MGA opinion

MGA is a different "day" definition, not a new engine. The cleanest path: add constructor params for the dawn/dusk minute offsets (e.g. 72), and a shaah_zmanis derivation that uses sunrise − 72min and sunset + 72min when MGA mode is on. Keep GRA as the default. All proportional formulas stay the same — only the hour length changes.

Expose opinion controls in the UI

Add sidebar widgets in app.py (number inputs for each angle/offset), then build the calculator from those values instead of the default-constructed one. Because ZmanimApp.__init__ already accepts an injected calculator, you can also wire this through dependency injection without changing the engine.

Add a location method

Extend LocationResolver.resolve with a new branch (and a matching try_parse_* helper). Respect the boundary-validation rule and, for any network call, the throttle pattern used for Nominatim. Add branch tests in tests/test_location_resolver.py (mock the network).

Do not
Don't change a depression angle, the / 12 proportional-hour divisor, or the candle/havdalah formulas without (a) confirming against MyZmanim.com / Chabad.org / KosherJava within the stated tolerance and (b) updating the reference test. This is a halachic tool — a silent off-by-a-few-minutes change can cause someone to miss a zman or mistime candle lighting.
Do not
Don't add FastAPI endpoints, user accounts, Hebrew-calendar logic, or notifications in this phase — CLAUDE.md §2 explicitly defers those to Phase 2/3. Keep Phase 1 a single-process Streamlit app.
Tip
After any change, run ruff check . and pytest. The coverage gate is 100% (--cov-fail-under=100), so new logic must come with tests or the suite fails locally and in CI.

How to run

  1. uv run pytest — runs the suite with coverage and the 100% gate (from addopts in pyproject.toml).
  2. uv run pytest --cov-report=term-missing — see exactly which lines aren't covered.
Testing rules (from CLAUDE.md §9)
No mocking of solar calculations — test against real astral. Mocking is allowed for Nominatim HTTP (via responses) and pgeocode. Numerical comparisons use real tolerances (zmanim should match reference sources within 1–2 minutes). Every engine method should have a reference-validated case; edge cases to cover include polar regions, equinox, solstice, and the date line.

Build, CI & deployment

CI is GitHub Actions (not GitLab, despite docs). Two workflows: continuous integration and a manual release/version-bump.

CI pipeline

Defined in .github/workflows/ci.yml, triggered on push/PR to main, staging, dev (and manual dispatch). Stages chain via needs::

JobCommandGate
lintruff check .Fail on any lint error.
testpytest --cov=src --cov-report=...Inherits the 100% gate from pyproject addopts.
builduv buildWheel/sdist must build; uploads dist/.
docker-buildbuildx (no push)Only on main/staging; GHA layer cache.

Release / versioning

.github/workflows/release.yml is a manual (workflow_dispatch) job on main that runs uv version --bump {patch|minor|major}, commits the new pyproject.toml, and tags+pushes. The version field there is the single source of truth (currently 0.2.0). Per CLAUDE.md §11, contributors document the next version in docs/versions.md but never hand-edit the version field — the release pipeline owns it.

Docker image

Dockerfile: python:3.13-slim base, installs deps with uv pip install --system -r pyproject.toml, copies source, exposes 5270, and runs Streamlit headless. .dockerignore trims the build context.

Doc vs. reality
CLAUDE.md §8 and the master plan describe a .gitlab-ci.yml with GitLab stages. The actual CI is GitHub Actions under .github/workflows/; no .gitlab-ci.yml exists in the tree. The stage intent (lint → test → build → docker-build) matches; only the platform differs.

Domain glossary

The halachic and astronomical terms a newcomer needs to read the code. Hebrew transliterations match the field names in ZmanimRow.

Zman (pl. zmanim)
A halachic time boundary computed from the sun's position; governs when prayers and observances may/must be done.
Shaah zmanis (pl. shaos zmanios)
A "proportional hour" — daylight divided into 12 equal parts. Longer in summer, shorter in winter. The unit for time-based zmanim. Field: shaah_zmanis.
GRA
The Vilna Gaon's opinion: the halachic day runs sunrise→sunset. The default opinion implemented here.
MGA
Magen Avraham's opinion: the day runs from 72 minutes before sunrise to 72 minutes after sunset (a longer day). Planned, not yet implemented.
Alos / Alos HaShachar
Dawn — first light. Computed at a depression angle (16.9° astronomical, 12° nautical). Fields: alos_astronomical_edge, alos_nautical_edge.
Misheyakir
The earliest time one can recognize a familiar acquaintance — earliest for tallit and tefillin. ~10° depression. Field: misheyakir.
Hanetz / Sunrise (Netz HaChama)
Visible sunrise; the start of the GRA halachic day. Field: sunrise.
Krias Shema
The Shema recitation; its latest time (Sof Zman Krias Shema) is sunrise + 3 shaos. Field: latest_shema.
Tefillah / Shacharit
Morning prayer; its latest time is sunrise + 4 shaos. Field: latest_shacharit.
Chatzos
Halachic midday = solar noon (= sunrise + 6 shaos). Field: chatzos / solar_noon.
Mincha Gedolah
Earliest Mincha; half a shaah after chatzos (sunrise + 6.5 shaos). Field: earliest_mincha.
Mincha Ketana
Preferred Mincha (sunrise + 9.5 shaos). Field: mincha_ketana.
Plag HaMincha
"Half of Mincha" — 1.25 shaos before sunset (sunrise + 10.75 shaos); the boundary for early Maariv / early Shabbat acceptance. Field: plag_hamincha.
Shkiah / Sunset
Visible sunset; the end of the GRA day. Field: sunset.
Tzais / Tzais HaKochavim
Nightfall — three medium stars visible (~8.5° depression). Marks the end of the day for many purposes, including havdalah. Fields: tzais_three_stars, tzais_civil_end.
Chatzot HaLaila
Halachic midnight — the midpoint between sunset and the next sunrise. Field: chatzot_halaila.
Candle lighting (Hadlakas Neiros)
Lighting Shabbat candles Friday, customarily 18 minutes before sunset (40 in Jerusalem). Field: candle_lighting.
Havdalah
Ceremony ending Shabbat Saturday night, at/after tzais. Field: shabbat_ends.
Shitah (pl. shitot)
A halachic opinion. Each shitah is a configuration of the calculator, not a separate engine.
Minhag
A custom (e.g. the local candle-lighting offset).
Depression angle
How far the sun sits below the horizon, in degrees; the input to dawn/dusk astronomy. Higher angle = darker = farther from sunrise/sunset.
Solar noon
The instant the sun is highest (transits the meridian); equals chatzos.
